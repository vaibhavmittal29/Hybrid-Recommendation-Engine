# Final Report: Hybrid Recommendation Engine with Cold-Start Handling

## 1. Approach

We engineered a **hybrid recommendation system** prioritizing cold-start resilience by adaptively blending Collaborative Filtering (CF) and Content-Based (CB) strategies.

### Models
- **Collaborative Filtering (CF)**: Matrix Factorization via Funk SVD (`scikit-surprise`). It builds dense user and item embeddings based on historical interactions. High capacity but fails entirely on cold items/users.
- **Content-Based Filtering (CB)**: TF-IDF vectorization over an engineered textual field comprising the item's `title`, `year`, `genres`, and `tags`. A user's profile is constructed by computing the weighted average cosine similarity across all their historically rated items. This is extremely robust to cold-start scenarios, relying purely on intrinsic item metadata.

### Adaptive Sigmoid Blending
Instead of a static mix, we implemented a sigmoid-based weighting function $\alpha(u)$:
$$ \alpha(u) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-0.5 (n_u - 10)}} $$
Where $n_u$ is the user's interaction count. For a cold user ($n_u < 5$), the CF weight drops below 8%, pushing the engine into an almost purely CB mode. For warm users ($n_u \ge 20$), CF dominates $> 99\%$. Both CF and CB outputs are Min-Max normalized on a per-user basis prior to blending to equalize scaling distributions.

## 2. Key Decisions

- **Temporal Train/Test Split**: We sorted interactions chronologically and partitioned the first 80% for training and the most recent 20% for testing. This realistically mimics deployment environments where future preferences are predicted.
- **Cold-Start Simulation**: Because ML-1M natively enforces $\ge 20$ ratings, we explicitly fabricated a cold-start slice by sampling 10% of training users and truncating their training history to $< 5$ interactions.
- **500-Negative Ranking Evaluation**: True ranking quality was evaluated by sampling 500 unobserved candidate items alongside true positive test items (rating $\ge 4.0$) per user, enabling rapid but statistically robust measurements of Precision@K, Recall@K, and NDCG@K.
- **MMR Re-ranking**: We implemented Maximal Marginal Relevance to penalize redundant candidates based on cosine similarity, improving catalog novelty and diversifying the top-10 list.
- **Popularity Baseline**: We explicitly compare our Hybrid engine against a global item-frequency baseline to quantify genuine personalization lift.

## 3. Results & Failure Analysis

*(For precise metrics, review `reports/metrics.txt` generated by running `evaluate.py`.)*

### Expected Efficacy
The Hybrid model is dynamically evaluated across **Warm Users**, **Cold-Start Users**, **Warm Items**, and **Cold Items**. During Cold-Start evaluations, while absolute scores natively drop compared to warm states, the system maintains robustness by defaulting gracefully to Content-Based similarities. This explicitly prevents complete failure states. We specifically track "Cold Items" (items with < 5 interactions in training), demonstrating that unseen catalog items still receive highly meaningful content-based scoring via TF-IDF, circumventing the classic CF limitation.

### Automated Failure Categorizations (`reports/failure_analysis_details.md`)
We instituted an evidence-backed categorization pipeline to flag and diagnose users achieving NDCG@10 = 0:
1. **Metadata Poverty**: Confirmed when the missing relevant items have extremely sparse or hyper-generic tags (e.g., an average of <= 1.5 genres). The Content-Based fallback fails to isolate these items due to a lack of descriptive metadata.
2. **Popularity Bias**: Confirmed when the median popularity of the top 10 recommended items falls into the top 10% of the most frequently interacted items globally, overpowering nuanced personalized recommendations.
3. **Sparse History**: The user's interaction count was explicitly too low to establish a clear pattern, yet neither popularity bias nor metadata poverty was the immediate structural cause.
4. **Other**: Taste drifts, anomalous behavior, or complex feature interactions where the model simply misses the mark despite adequate metadata and interaction density.
