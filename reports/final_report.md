# Final Report: Hybrid Recommendation Engine with Cold-Start Handling

## 1. Approach

We engineered a **hybrid recommendation system** prioritizing cold-start resilience by adaptively blending Collaborative Filtering (CF) and Content-Based (CB) strategies.

### Models
- **Collaborative Filtering (CF)**: Matrix Factorization via Funk SVD (`scikit-surprise`). It builds dense user and item embeddings based on historical interactions. High capacity but fails entirely on cold items/users.
- **Content-Based Filtering (CB)**: TF-IDF vectorization over an engineered textual field comprising the item's `title`, `year`, `genres`, and `tags`. A user's profile is constructed by computing the weighted average cosine similarity across items they have interacted with, strictly filtering for **positive preferences (rating $\ge$ 3.0)**. Items rated below 3.0 are discarded and do not influence the content profile. This fallback is extremely robust to cold-start scenarios, relying purely on intrinsic item metadata.

### Adaptive Sigmoid Blending
Instead of a static mix, we implemented a sigmoid-based weighting function $\alpha(u)$:
$$ \alpha(u) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-0.5 (n_u - 10)}} $$
Where $n_u$ is the user's interaction count. For a cold user ($n_u < 5$), the CF weight drops below 8%, pushing the engine into an almost purely CB mode. For warm users ($n_u \ge 20$), CF dominates $> 99\%$. Both CF and CB outputs are Min-Max normalized on a per-user basis prior to blending to equalize scaling distributions.

## 2. Key Decisions

- **Temporal Train/Test Split**: We sorted interactions chronologically and partitioned the first 80% for training and the most recent 20% for testing. This realistically mimics deployment environments where future preferences are predicted.
- **Cold-Start Simulation & Slicing**: Because ML-1M natively enforces $\ge 20$ ratings per user, we explicitly fabricated a **cold-user slice** by randomly sampling 10% of users and truncating their training history to fewer than 5 interactions. We also defined **cold items** strictly as items possessing fewer than 5 interactions within the training split, allowing us to accurately track recommendation viability for sparse catalog entries without train/test leakage.
- **500-Negative Ranking Evaluation**: True ranking quality was evaluated by sampling 500 unobserved candidate items alongside true positive test items (rating $\ge 4.0$) per user, enabling rapid but statistically robust measurements of Precision@K, Recall@K, and NDCG@K.
- **Popularity Baseline**: We explicitly compare our Hybrid engine against a global item-frequency baseline to honestly benchmark ranking quality and personalization.

### Performance Gap & Limitations

As measured, the Hybrid model (NDCG@10 = 0.1602) currently underperforms the naive Popularity Baseline (NDCG@10 = 0.2740). 

**Honest Assessment & Potential Improvements:**
1. **Candidate Generation**: The current evaluator ranks 500 randomly drawn negative items along with the target. A globally popular item has a naturally higher likelihood of being universally accepted by users, meaning the baseline easily sweeps high NDCG scores. The hybrid model lacks a multi-stage retrieval funnel (e.g., retrieving popular items first, then re-ranking), attempting to rank purely on raw cosine/latent similarity across disparate candidates.
2. **Content-Model Features**: The TF-IDF model relies primarily on movie genres and titles. Movie metadata in ML-1M is highly sparse; a genre match is often too generic to determine true thematic taste, severely limiting the CB model's precision for cold users and cold items. Expanding metadata features (e.g., cast, directors, full plot summaries) via a Sentence-BERT embedding would drastically improve content resolution.
3. **Adaptive-Weight Tuning**: The sigmoid curve limits were chosen heuristically. A more rigorous hyperparameter search on the blending function parameters ($\tau$, $\beta$) could better optimize the handoff between CB and CF.
4. **Cold-Item Ranking**: Cold items (NDCG@10 = 0.0239) predictably struggle to surface against densely interacted items in a normalized score pool. Future iterations should implement a targeted exploration bandit or explicit content-based candidate boosting to equalize the playing field for cold items.

### Automated Failure Categorizations (`reports/failure_analysis_details.md`)
We instituted an evidence-backed categorization pipeline to flag and diagnose users achieving NDCG@10 = 0:
1. **Metadata Poverty**: Confirmed when the missing relevant items have extremely sparse or hyper-generic tags (e.g., an average of $\le 1.5$ genres). 
2. **Popularity Bias**: Confirmed when the median popularity of the top 10 recommended items falls into the top 10% of the most frequently interacted items globally.
3. **Sparse History**: The user's interaction count was explicitly too low to establish a clear pattern.
4. **Other**: Taste drifts, anomalous behavior, or complex feature interactions where the model simply misses the mark despite adequate metadata and interaction density.
